Aircraft Logistics Management System (US Air Force)

Stack:

View Code

  • Python
  • Html5
  • Css3
  • JavaScript

Aircraft Logistics Management is a Django web application built for Tesseract, an Air Force logistics team. My team of three built it from scratch, from the design to the finished features, and I led the team and mentored the other two developers. The app schedules a fleet of aircraft, tracks when each one needs its next maintenance, shows upcoming maintenance and deployments on a calendar, and manages the parts inventory.

Results

  • Cut the team's manual work by 40% by importing their old maintenance logs into the app.
  • Reduced user errors by 75% by redesigning the data-entry forms.
  • Documented the REST APIs in Swagger/OpenAPI.

How we built it

We worked in three phases. First, we met with the Air Force organizers to understand what they needed, and refined the design with their feedback. Then we built the app in Agile sprints, with regular check-ins as a team. Finally, we walked the organizers through the finished app and added the feature they asked for most.

We chose Django because it scales well, which mattered for an app meant to run across many Air Force bases. Python did the heavy lifting on the data side: a CSV import pulls the old maintenance logs into the database tables.

Challenges

The hardest part was the old maintenance records. They were full of data we didn't need, so we wrote a parser that pulls out only the useful fields before loading them into the database. We also built the scheduling logic around the Air Force's Theory of Constraints methodology.

What I learned

This was my first large Django project, so I learned a lot about structuring and organizing code. I also saw how much smoother a project runs with Agile: short sprints, regular check-ins, and steady feedback from the people who will use the app.
