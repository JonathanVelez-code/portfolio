Hotel Booking Cancellation & Revenue Analysis

Stack:

View Code

  • pandas
  • XGBoost
  • scikit-learn
  • SQL
  • DuckDB
  • Power BI

How much revenue do cancellations cost two real hotels, which bookings are most likely to cancel, and what should revenue managers do about it? This project uses the public Hotel Booking Demand dataset: 119,390 real bookings from a resort hotel and a city hotel in Portugal, for arrivals from July 2015 to August 2017.

What it does

  • Analyzes cancellations and lost revenue in SQL with DuckDB, by hotel, booking window, segment and month.
  • Predicts cancellations with logistic regression and XGBoost, using only what's known when the booking is made. Columns that leak the outcome, such as reservation status, assigned room and country, are left out.
  • Validates by time: trains on arrivals through December 2016 and tests on January to August 2017.
  • Turns the predictions into decisions: which bookings to target, how many rooms to overbook safely, and where a deposit would help.

Results

  • 37% of bookings cancelled, about 16.7M in booked room revenue.
  • City Hotel cancellations rise from 12% for bookings made within a week to 71% for bookings made a year or more ahead.
  • XGBoost ROC AUC of 0.83, vs. 0.82 for logistic regression and 0.66 for a booking-window rule.
  • The riskiest 20% of bookings hold 44% of all cancellations.
  • The weekly cancellation forecast has 12.7% error vs. 16.1% for historical averages. A 95%-confidence overbooking level would have overshot in only 2.8% of hotel-weeks.

Limits

It covers two hotels in one country from 2015 to 2017, so the patterns may not carry over elsewhere. The forecast ran low in spring 2017, when cancellations rose above 2016 levels, and the value of a deposit policy can only be proven with a real test.
